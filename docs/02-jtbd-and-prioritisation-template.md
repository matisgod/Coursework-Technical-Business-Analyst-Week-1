## Step 4: Top 3 justification


**JTBD-02**

Why it matters now: Legacy-Trust Bank's recovery operation cannot scale because agent capacity is artificially capped by compounding administration duties. Agents are spending 20–22.5 minutes per case regardless of its complexity or risk tier because they are forced to manually parse and collate data across disconnected data sources. With a volume exceeding 100,000 cases, the bank is facing a hard ceiling where they must linearly add headcount just to maintain the current volume of open cases. Solving this immediately unlocks trapped capacity.

Which evidence supports it: The smart recovery portal events suggests that recovery activity is evenly split across completely disconnected source systems (spreadsheets, email trails, and CRM notes). Accessing an account depends on individual memorization and manual extraction, which directly leads to duplicate activities.

How it should influence Phase 1: Phase 1 must focus heavily on establishing a single, unified backend data layer that aggregates a customer's balance, communication history, and case status into one screen. Before building advanced agent tooling, the platform must eliminate the multi-system hopping and manual data collation. This foundational step is critical to establish the pre-transformation baseline that product and delivery teams need.

**JTBD-05**

Why it matters now: Operations leadership wants a clear distinction between simple, trivial cases and complex cases requiring human judgment. Currently, agents are spending the same amount of time on trivial balance queries as they are on complex negotiations. By failing to provide a self-serve portal for routine tasks, the bank is over-allocating expensive, specialized human capital to low-value work, capping the overall recovery rate percentage. To hit measurable results within 12 months, simple cases must be systematically offloaded.

Which evidence supports it: Customers are pushed into "slow, repetitive contact cycles" and express anxiety over uncertainty regarding whether their balances or actions are correct. Leadership has explicitly stated a preference to let a self-serve portal handle trivial cases (such as understanding what they owe, viewing available payment options, and checking case status).

How it should influence Phase 1: The external portal architecture in Phase 1 must prioritize high-frequency, low-complexity self-service capabilities. The initial release should focus on exposing accurate balances, visible repayment options, and instant confirmation of completed actions. This will immediately deflect routine administrative traffic away from phone and email queues.

**JTBD-06**

Why it matters now: Because there is no separation between accounts based on risk tier, delinquency stage, or complexity, high-value or high-risk accounts are getting lost in the noise. This lack of triage causes missed follow-ups and inconsistent tracking, directly dragging down the bank's collection performance. Furthermore, team leaders lack the real-time visibility needed to spot these bottlenecks until the end of the week, forcing them into a reactive management posture.

Which evidence supports it: The recovery activity tracker shows 20% duplicate activities and highlights a systemic problem with "missed follow-ups". Team leaders worry about an "all-or-nothing" system that leaves awkward or "messy cases landing with agents who have even less context than before".

How it should influence Phase 1: Phase 1 must implement a programmatic triage engine and clear escalation paths. The system needs to automatically flag high-risk or high-value cases for immediate agent intervention, while maintaining a seamless hand-off path from the self-serve portal to an agent when a customer's situation becomes complex. This directly answers leadership's requirement for a well-defined future-state process with realistic hand-offs for messy cases.
